Rubber 101

Natural vs. synthetic rubber

Every rubber behaves differently with heat, oil, weather and wear. Choosing the right compound is the single biggest factor in how long your part lasts.

🌿 Natural Rubber

Harvested as latex from the Hevea brasiliensis tree. It offers outstanding elasticity, tensile strength and tear resistance, making it ideal for dynamic, high-wear uses. However, it degrades with sunlight, ozone and oils.

⚗️ Synthetic Rubber

Engineered from petrochemicals to deliver specific properties such as oil resistance (Nitrile), weather resistance (EPDM), extreme heat tolerance (Silicone, FKM) or low gas permeability (Butyl). Most industrial seals today are synthetic.

FAQ

Common questions

What does "Shore A hardness" mean?

Shore A is the standard scale for measuring rubber hardness. Lower numbers are softer (around 30 is like a rubber band), 60–70 is typical for gaskets and seals, and 90 is very firm, similar to a shoe heel. If you're unsure, tell us the application and we'll recommend a hardness.

EPDM or Neoprene: which should I choose for outdoor use?

EPDM generally has better resistance to sunlight, ozone, weathering and hot water, making it the first choice for roofing, window seals and outdoor gaskets. Choose Neoprene if you also need moderate oil resistance or flame retardancy.

Do you offer food-grade or FDA-compliant rubber?

Yes. Silicone, EPDM, Nitrile and Natural Rubber can be supplied in food-grade formulations. Please mention the compliance standard you need (for example FDA, EC 1935/2004 or WRAS) in your inquiry.

Can you manufacture parts from my drawing or sample?

Absolutely. Send us your drawing, dimensions or details of a sample along with the material, hardness and quantity required, and we'll come back with a quotation and lead time.

What is your minimum order quantity and lead time?

This depends on the product. Stock sheets and standard O-rings can ship quickly in small quantities, while custom moulded parts may need tooling. Include your required quantity in the form below and we'll confirm both.
